## Political considerations

Look for:

- contested interests;
    
- governance legitimacy;
    
- competing values;
    
- historical disputes;
    
- incentives;
    
- coalition formation;
    
- constitutional boundaries;
    
- distribution of costs and benefits.
    

Do not label politics as noise surrounding an otherwise technical standard.

Standards frequently redistribute power by changing what can be seen, compared, required or automated.

## Institutional and syndication presence

Identify actors whose position allows them to aggregate or propagate behavior across the ecosystem.

Examples may include:

- governance institutions;
    
- major tooling platforms;
    
- standards bodies;
    
- exchanges;
    
- wallets;
    
- repositories;
    
- networks;
    
- associations;
    
- media;
    
- major delegations.
    

A syndicating actor can cause a convention to become effectively universal without formal ecosystem-wide agreement.

## Control

Ask explicitly:

- Who can change this system?
    
- Who can refuse change?
    
- Who controls interfaces?
    
- Who controls canonical records?
    
- Who controls funding?
    
- Who controls access?
    
- Who controls implementation?
    
- Who can fork?
    
- Who can leave?
    

Record control as a relationship rather than automatically assigning it to an organisation.

## Working pattern: Locate the veto and the exit

For each important relationship ask:

> Who can say no?

and:

> Who can leave?

Those two questions frequently reveal more about governance topology than organisational charts do.

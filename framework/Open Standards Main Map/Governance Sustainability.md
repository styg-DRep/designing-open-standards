A standard that succeeds will eventually need to answer:

- Who can change it?
    
- Who decides what constitutes an error?
    
- Who evaluates extensions?
    
- How are conflicting needs handled?
    
- How are maintainers replaced?
    
- How are releases declared?
    
- What happens when consensus fails?
    
- How can governance itself change?
    

Governance should be proportional to the standard's maturity and consequences.

---

# Pattern: Governance Should Grow With the Burden

Start no heavier than necessary.

Possible progression:

```text
named steward
      ↓
small maintainer group
      ↓
open working group
      ↓
formalized change process
      ↓
institutional stewardship
```

Do not build elaborate governance for a standard with three experimental users.

But do not allow ecosystem-critical infrastructure to remain indefinitely dependent on one informal maintainer.

## Intent

Run a review as a bounded, recorded process — from confirming the trigger to producing findings — that separates reviewing from deciding.

# Process for Review

## Step 1 — Confirm the Trigger

State:

- why review began;
    
- who initiated it;
    
- what evidence or event triggered it;
    
- whether urgency applies.
    

---

## Step 2 — Define Review Scope

Identify:

- what is being reviewed;
    
- what is not;
    
- what decisions the review is allowed to recommend.
    

---

## Step 3 — Define Review Roles

Identify:

- who gathers evidence;
    
- who performs analysis;
    
- who facilitates deliberation;
    
- who makes the final decision;
    
- who is consulted;
    
- who may object.
    

Where possible, separate:

**reviewing**

from

**deciding**.

---

## Step 4 — Gather Evidence

Draw from:

- Launch and Adoption Outcomes;
    
- adopter interviews;
    
- support records;
    
- test results;
    
- change requests;
    
- implementation records;
    
- ecosystem maps;
    
- actor maps;
    
- theory-of-change updates;
    
- governance records;
    
- sustainability data;
    
- external policy or standards changes.
    

---

## Step 5 — Revisit the Baselines

Compare against:

- Scope and Start Bundle;
    
- Development Outcomes Bundle;
    
- Launch and Adoption Outcomes Bundle.
    

This preserves the lifecycle trajectory.

---

## Step 6 — Prioritize Review Questions

Do not allow a review to become an unstructured backlog discussion.

Prioritize questions based on:

- severity;
    
- frequency;
    
- ecosystem impact;
    
- strategic importance;
    
- reversibility;
    
- evidence quality;
    
- adopter burden.
    

---

## Step 7 — Deliberate Options

For important findings, consider more than one response.

Examples:

- no change;
    
- documentation fix;
    
- tooling improvement;
    
- minor update;
    
- major update;
    
- governance change;
    
- scope refinement;
    
- alignment with another standard;
    
- deprecation;
    
- retirement.
    

---

## Step 8 — Assess Consequences

For every proposed change ask:

- Who benefits?
    
- Who bears migration cost?
    
- What breaks?
    
- What must be retrained?
    
- What infrastructure changes?
    
- What policy or institutional dependencies are affected?
    
- What does this do to interoperability?
    

---

## Step 9 — Record Dissent

Preserve:

- objections;
    
- minority views;
    
- uncertainties;
    
- contested evidence;
    
- unresolved risks.
    

---

## Step 10 — Produce Findings

The review should conclude with:

- findings;
    
- evidence basis;
    
- confidence;
    
- recommended next-step options;
    
- unresolved questions.
    

---

# Pattern: Review the Standard and the System Around It

A standard may remain internally coherent while becoming externally obsolete.

Review:

- the artifact;
    
- the use cases;
    
- the adopters;
    
- the infrastructure;
    
- adjacent standards;
    
- governance;
    
- ecosystem conditions.

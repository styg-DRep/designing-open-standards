## Intent

Determine whether the review has produced enough evidence and sufficiently clear findings to support a consequential lifecycle decision.

The gate asks:

> **Do we understand the present fitness of the standard well enough to decide what should happen next?**

---

# Review Outcomes Bundle

The bundle should include:

- review trigger;
    
- review scope;
    
- participants;
    
- evidence considered;
    
- findings;
    
- confidence;
    
- unresolved disputes;
    
- ecosystem changes;
    
- theory-of-change findings;
    
- sustainability findings;
    
- change requests;
    
- impact of possible responses;
    
- next-step options.
    

---

# Fitness Assessment

A useful high-level classification might be:

## Fit

The standard remains appropriate and effective.

## Fit With Maintenance

The standard remains appropriate but needs minor corrections, documentation, tooling, or support.

## Fit With Update

Substantive changes are required, but the core development case remains valid.

## Requires Reframing

The standard's original opportunity, aims, or scope no longer sufficiently describe the problem.

## Superseded

Another standard, system, or practice now better fulfills the need.

## Unsustainable

The standard may still be valuable but cannot credibly be maintained in its current form.

## Harmful / Misaligned

Evidence suggests the standard produces unacceptable consequences or no longer serves its intended purpose.

---

# Gate Criteria

The review is complete enough to move to next-step decision-making when:

1. the trigger has been addressed;
    
2. relevant evidence has been collected;
    
3. original aims and use cases have been reconsidered;
    
4. actual adoption and impact are understood well enough;
    
5. major defects and requested changes are prioritized;
    
6. ecosystem changes are visible;
    
7. sustainability is assessed;
    
8. affected adopters have been considered;
    
9. major disagreements are documented;
    
10. plausible next-step options have been evaluated.
    

---

# Possible Outcomes

## Proceed to Decide Next Steps

The review is sufficiently complete.

## Return to Review Process

Evidence is weak or important perspectives are missing.

## Return to Monitoring

Real-world evidence remains too thin.

## Return to Scope

The review reveals that the underlying problem, aim, or intervention requires reframing.

## Emergency Action

A serious defect or harm requires immediate mitigation before full review completion.

---

# Pattern: Review Produces Options, Not Automatic Change

Review should not automatically lead to an update.

Its job is to produce a defensible assessment and a bounded set of next-step options.

The next cluster converts that assessment into action.

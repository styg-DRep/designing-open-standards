## Intent

Observe how the standard is actually being used and how the ecosystem changes around it.

Monitoring should answer more than:

> How many adopters do we have?

It should ask:

> **Who is using the standard, how, with what effects, under what conditions, and with what unexpected consequences?**

---

# Direct Adoption

Track actors who directly use or implement the standard.

Possible evidence:

- known implementations;
    
- registrations;
    
- integrations;
    
- repository dependencies;
    
- validator use;
    
- public declarations;
    
- participation in support spaces.
    

Avoid assuming that unregistered use does not exist.

---

# Indirect Adoption

Look for use downstream from direct implementation.

Examples:

- tools consuming standardized governance information;
    
- researchers depending on standardized records;
    
- institutional procedures incorporating the standard;
    
- another standard reusing the vocabulary;
    
- governance participants relying on standardized outputs without knowing the standard by name.
    

Indirect use can be more important than direct adoption counts.

---

# Pattern: Monitor Behavior, Not Just Reach

Distinguish:

```text
awareness
↓
experimentation
↓
adoption
↓
routine use
↓
dependency
↓
ecosystem integration
```

Ten organizations trying a standard is different from ten organizations becoming operationally dependent on it.

---

# Use-Case Monitoring

Return to the original use cases.

For each ask:

- Is the use case occurring?
    
- Is it occurring as expected?
    
- Are new use cases emerging?
    
- Are important anticipated use cases absent?
    
- Are adopters modifying the standard locally?
    
- Are workarounds becoming common?
    

Unexpected use cases may reveal valuable extension paths.

Workarounds may reveal defects.

---

# Pattern: Watch the Forks

Pay particular attention when adopters:

- redefine terminology;
    
- add incompatible fields;
    
- ignore requirements;
    
- create proprietary extensions;
    
- fork guidance;
    
- build transformation layers.
    

These may indicate:

- missing extensibility;
    
- excessive rigidity;
    
- unmet use cases;
    
- unclear requirements;
    
- legitimate domain variation.
    

Do not automatically treat divergence as misuse.

It may be ecosystem feedback.

---

# Impact Monitoring

Reconnect adoption to the Theory of Change.

Ask:

- Are the expected mechanisms occurring?
    
- Are the intended outcomes visible?
    
- Are actors behaving differently?
    
- Is coordination easier?
    
- Is interoperability improving?
    
- Are unintended effects appearing?
    

This separates:

> adoption success

from

> impact success.

A widely adopted standard may still fail its intended purpose.

---

# Refresh the Actor Map

Update:

- new adopters;
    
- new implementers;
    
- new intermediaries;
    
- new maintainers;
    
- actors who left;
    
- emerging functional roles.
    

---

# Refresh the Ecosystem Map

Look for changes in:

- infrastructure;
    
- information flows;
    
- standards;
    
- institutions;
    
- dependencies;
    
- control;
    
- power;
    
- interoperability;
    
- competing standards.
    

The standard itself may now have become part of the infrastructure it originally mapped.

That is a critical transition.

---

# Pattern: Map the Standard Back Into the Ecosystem

During Scope, the ecosystem map asks:

> Where would this standard operate?

After adoption, ask:

> **What position has the standard actually taken within the ecosystem?**

Is it:

- peripheral guidance?
    
- shared vocabulary?
    
- common interface?
    
- de facto infrastructure?
    
- institutional requirement?
    
- dependency?
    
- contested coordination point?
    

That answer should strongly shape Review.

---

# Refresh the Theory of Change

Compare the original theory with observed reality.

```text
Expected mechanism
        versus
Observed mechanism

Expected outcome
        versus
Observed outcome

Expected adopters
        versus
Actual adopters

Expected impact
        versus
Observed impact
```

Update:

- assumptions;
    
- causal links;
    
- enabling conditions;
    
- unintended effects.
    

---

# Pattern: Preserve Longitudinal Scoping Artifacts

Do not overwrite the Scope artifacts and lose history.

Maintain:

```text
Scope baseline
      ↓
Launch snapshot
      ↓
Review snapshot
```

This allows later reviewers to see not only the current ecosystem, but how understanding changed.

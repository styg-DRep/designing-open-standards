## Intent

Keep development legible to the communities that may use, implement, or be affected by the standard, while creating repeated opportunities for those communities to alter the work.

---

# Pattern: Share for Response

A progress update should make clear:

- what changed;
    
- why it changed;
    
- what remains unresolved;
    
- what is currently being tested;
    
- what feedback is needed;
    
- how and by when someone can respond.
    

Do not merely say:

> Version 0.4 released.

Say:

> Version 0.4 changes the proposal-status model because testing exposed ambiguity between enacted and implemented actions. We particularly need feedback from indexers and governance historians before the next candidate.

---

# Pattern: Milestones Are Invitations

Important milestones should create visible participation windows.

Examples:

- conceptual model review;
    
- vocabulary freeze;
    
- schema candidate;
    
- first interoperable implementations;
    
- candidate release;
    
- breaking-change review.
    

A milestone is not just project reporting.

It is a structured moment at which the wider ecosystem can still matter.

---

# Pattern: Report Disagreement

Progress reports should include:

- unresolved objections;
    
- competing alternatives;
    
- decisions deferred;
    
- important test failures.
    

A healthy open process should not appear artificially unanimous.

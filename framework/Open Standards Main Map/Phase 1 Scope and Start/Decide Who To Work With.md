## Intent

Identify people able to participate meaningfully in the next stages of development.

Do not collapse this into a single category called “early adopters.”

Different relationships to the work matter.

### Shapers

People who can improve the standard through:

- needs;
    
- critique;
    
- requests;
    
- domain knowledge;
    
- lived experience;
    
- implementation constraints.
    

They do not need to support the current proposal.

Good shapers may be persistent critics.

### Testers and early adopters

People able and willing to encounter early forms of the standard in actual practice.

They help discover whether the proposed agreement survives reality.

### Advocates and ambassadors

People capable of translating the effort into other communities and helping others understand why participation or adoption may be worthwhile.

Advocacy should emerge from confidence in the work, not substitute for testing it.

## Pattern: Build a cohort, not an audience

The goal at this stage is not to accumulate supporters.

Seek a small enough group to work with meaningfully but diverse enough to challenge the assumptions embedded in the effort.

A useful cohort contains:

- people who need the proposed capability;
    
- people who would implement it;
    
- people affected indirectly;
    
- people with relevant infrastructure knowledge;
    
- people likely to disagree;
    
- people capable of testing it.

This node is slightly less a category of people and more a search for **information intermediaries and exchange relationships**.

The core question is:

**Who creates, transforms, carries, interprets, republishes, validates, or consumes governance information as it moves through the ecosystem?**

Governance information can include:

- proposals;
- metadata;
- voting intentions;
- rationales;
- deliberative records;
- constitutional interpretations;
- recommendations;
- research;
- implementation status;
- treasury information;
- standards and specifications;
- identity and delegation information;
- historical governance records.

The people involved in exchange are often not the same as the original producers.

For example, a proposer may produce a proposal, while another actor:

- indexes it;
- summarizes it;
- translates it;
- republishes it;
- validates its metadata;
- comments on it;
- compares it with other proposals;
- feeds it into another tool;
- archives it.

A good discovery technique is to choose one governance object and **follow its transformations**.

For example:

`proposal authoring → submission → indexing → display → commentary → summary → deliberation → vote → archival record`

At each step ask:

- Who handles the information?
- Does its format change?
- Does its meaning change?
- Is anything discarded?
- Is anything added?
- Who decides what becomes visible?
- Who decides what is canonical?
- Can the next actor verify where the information came from?

A useful working pattern is:

**Information object → transformation → intermediary → recipient**

For example:

`governance proposal → summary → community analyst → voters`

`voting data → aggregation → dashboard provider → researchers`

#### Things to capture

For each exchange relationship, record:

- sender or source;
- recipient;
- information object;
- format;
- channel;
- transformation;
- frequency;
- whether provenance survives;
- whether the exchange is machine-readable;
- whether the exchange is open;
- whether the recipient can verify the source;
- where interpretation or filtering occurs.

A particularly useful question is:

> **Where does governance information change hands and cease to be the same thing?**

That is often where standards are most valuable.

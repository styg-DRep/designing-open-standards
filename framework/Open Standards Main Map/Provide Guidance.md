## Intent

Create repeatable guidance for performing governance work consistently without necessarily requiring identical outputs.

Guidance standards are useful when variability in **practice** rather than vocabulary or representation is the principal coordination problem.

We typically describe these as frameworks and recommendations for understanding information flows and performing processes consistently, including protocols, methods, measures, and codes of practice.

## Governance-native forms

A guidance standard might define:

- proposal-development practices;
    
- deliberation protocols;
    
- evidence-assessment methods;
    
- consultation practices;
    
- review procedures;
    
- governance audit practices;
    
- stewardship methods;
    
- transparency expectations;
    
- change-management procedures;
    
- codes of governance practice.
    

## Discovery questions

Ask:

- Is the core problem inconsistent behavior rather than incompatible information?
    
- Do actors need a repeatable method?
    
- Is consistency desirable without requiring identical outcomes?
    
- Is the important artifact a process, protocol, or recommendation?
    
- Would rigid machine validation be inappropriate?
